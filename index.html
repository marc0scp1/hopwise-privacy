Hopwise Privacy Policy

Last updated: 9 November 2026

In short: Hopwise does not collect, send or share any personal data. It has no account, no ads, no analytics, no telemetry, no crash reporting and no backend server. Everything you enter stays on your device. The location permission, if you grant it, is used only on your device to read Wi-Fi names; your location is never stored or sent.

Who we are

Hopwise (package com.hopwise.app) is a network diagnostic app for Android, published on Google Play by the developer “Hopwise”. This policy explains how the app handles information.

Data we collect

None. The developer does not receive any data from the app: no personal data, no usage data, no device identifiers, no diagnostic results. The app does not use an advertising ID.

Data stored on your device

The sites, devices, hosts, favorites, diagnostic history, baselines and settings you create are stored only on your device, in a local SQLite database that belongs to the app. Android cloud backup and device-to-device transfer are disabled for this data, so it is not copied to Google or to another device by the app.

What the Wi-Fi tab shows (the path to the internet, signal, devices found and nearby networks) is kept in memory while the app is open and is never saved; only a diagnosis you start from a device found is saved in the history, like any other diagnosis. Two Wi-Fi settings are saved with the other settings: whether to check the internet and public IP, and whether you have already seen the warning before the first device search.

What traffic the app generates and where

Hopwise is a diagnostic tool, so it sends network traffic, but only where the diagnostics you run require. None of it goes to the developer.

Diagnostics and tools

  • The hosts you test (ping, traceroute, TCP ports, TLS, HTTP).
  • The DNS resolvers you choose: your system resolver, Cloudflare (1.1.1.1), Google (8.8.8.8), Quad9 (9.9.9.9), DNS over HTTPS to Cloudflare or Google, or a custom server you enter.
  • WHOIS servers, when you run a WHOIS lookup: whois.iana.org first, then the registry it refers to.

Wi-Fi tab: the path to the internet

When you open the Wi-Fi tab, when you come back to the app with the tab open, when you refresh it, when you turn the internet check back on, and when the phone joins another Wi-Fi network while the app is in the foreground, the app checks the path from your phone to the router and to the internet. It sends these checks over the Wi-Fi network even if mobile data is your default connection. If Android does not allow that (for example, with a VPN without bypass), they go out over your default connection (the VPN or mobile data) and the app says so on screen:

  • Your router (the Wi-Fi network's gateway): a few pings.
  • Cloudflare (1.1.1.1) and Google (8.8.8.8): a few pings each, to see whether the internet answers.
  • OpenDNS, run by Cisco (208.67.222.222): one DNS query for myip.opendns.com, which returns your network's public IP address.

These three services only see your network's public IP address, as with any connection. The app sends them no network name, access point, location or device information.

You can turn this off in Settings → Wi-Fi → Check internet and public IP. When it is off, the path stops at the router and Cloudflare, Google and OpenDNS are not contacted.

Wi-Fi tab: device search

Only when you tap Find devices, and only on networks you own or are authorized to test (the app warns you before the first search), the app searches the Wi-Fi network's subnet (at most 254 addresses) over the Wi-Fi network:

  • A ping to each address; a TCP connection attempt to 3 common ports (80, 443 and 445) on the addresses that do not answer the ping; and up to 18 common ports on each device found, including remote access ports such as Telnet and remote desktop, so the app can warn when they are open.
  • Device names: mDNS queries (multicast to 224.0.0.251 and to each device) and NetBIOS name queries to each device. This traffic stays on your local network.
  • Reverse DNS: for devices without a name, a reverse lookup of their local address sent to the router's DNS server. Some routers forward these lookups to their upstream DNS server, usually your internet provider's.

If Android does not let the app use the Wi-Fi network (for example, a VPN without bypass), the search sends nothing. The results are not saved. A diagnosis you start from a device found is saved in the history, like any other.

Wi-Fi tab: nearby networks

When you open Nearby networks or tap Scan, the app asks Android to scan for Wi-Fi networks. The scan is done by the phone's radio; the app reads the results (network name, access point, band, channel, security and signal), shows them and does not save or send them.

Third parties that receive traffic (the hosts you test, DNS resolvers, WHOIS servers, Cloudflare, Google, OpenDNS and your provider's DNS) see what any network request reveals, such as your IP address and the name or address being queried, and handle it under their own policies.

Location permission

Android only lets an app read the name of the Wi-Fi network (SSID), the access point (BSSID) and nearby networks if it has the precise location permission and location is turned on. Hopwise asks for it only when you tap the button in the Wi-Fi tab, and the rest of the app works without it.

  • It is used only on your device, to read the network name, access point and nearby networks.
  • The app never reads your GPS position or coordinates.
  • Nothing obtained with it (location, network names, access points) is stored or sent anywhere.
  • It is used only while the app is open, never in the background.

Exports and sharing

The app exports data (JSON backups, PDF or text reports, and an empty CSV import template) only when you choose to, through the Android share sheet or file picker, to a destination you pick. What happens to an exported file is up to you and the app or service you send it to.

Rating and feedback

  • “Rate Hopwise” opens the app’s page on Google Play. After a number of successful checks the app may also show Google Play’s in-app rating prompt (In-App Review), at most once every few months. Both are handled by Google Play under Google’s privacy policy; the developer receives only the public rating and review you choose to post.
  • “Send feedback” opens your own email app with a mailto: link. Nothing is sent unless you send the email yourself.

Permissions

  • Internet (android.permission.INTERNET): to send pings, DNS queries, TCP/TLS/HTTP probes and WHOIS lookups.
  • Network state (android.permission.ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE): to know whether the device is online, so the app can say “no network” instead of showing a misleading failure, and to send the Wi-Fi tab’s checks over the Wi-Fi network.
  • Wi-Fi state (android.permission.ACCESS_WIFI_STATE): to read the Wi-Fi connection (signal, band, channel, speed, addresses) and the last scan results.
  • Change Wi-Fi state (android.permission.CHANGE_WIFI_STATE): Android requires it to start a scan for nearby networks. The app does not change your Wi-Fi settings or connect to networks.
  • Precise and approximate location (android.permission.ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION, android.permission.ACCESS_COARSE_LOCATION): optional, see “Location permission” above.

The app does not request background location, contacts, storage, camera, phone, microphone or advertising ID permissions.

Deleting your data

Because all data is stored only on your device, you control it fully. Settings → Delete all data deletes every site, device, baseline, run, setting and the copies of shared reports and backups kept in the app’s cache. You can also clear the app’s storage in Android settings, or uninstall the app, which removes all its data. There is no copy on any server to delete.

Children

Hopwise is a tool for network professionals. It is not directed to children under 13 and is not designed for children. It does not knowingly collect information from anyone, including children.
